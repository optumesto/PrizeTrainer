How Standard Rotation Works

Most cards carry a regulation mark, a letter in the bottom corner. Once a year, the oldest legal mark leaves Standard. Recent years followed one pattern: D left in 2023, E in 2024, F in 2025 and G in 2026, each announced in January and gone from events in April.

What a Regulation Mark Is

The regulation mark is the small letter in a white box near the bottom left of most cards printed since 2020. Every card from the same block of sets shares a letter, and the letters run in order: D, E, F, G, H, I and on. Standard is the format used at most events, and it allows only the newest few letters. When a rotation happens, every card with the oldest legal letter leaves Standard at once, whatever its set.

Two kinds of card do not follow the letter. Basic Energy cards carry no regulation mark and stay legal in Standard. A card with an older mark is still legal when a reprint with the same name, attacks and Abilities carries a legal mark, so a favorite printing of a staple can stay in your deck after its own letter rotates.

Standard and Expanded

Standard is the rotating format that most local events, online ladders and Play! Pokémon championships use. Expanded is a larger format that keeps every card from the Black & White series onward, so rotation does not touch it in the same way. The rotation check on this page is for Standard.

How to Check Your List Before an Event

  1. Paste your list from Pokémon TCG Live or Limitless into RdyDeck, or open a deck you saved.
  2. Open the deck. Cards that are not legal now are marked, and a deck with any of them grades 4.9 at most until they are swapped.
  3. Look at the cards marked as may rotate next. They are fine today, but plan replacements if your event falls after the next rotation date.
  4. Check the official Play! Pokémon Rules and Formats page for the event date, since the official list is the final word.
  5. Grade the deck again after any swap. The deck checker shows whether the new card still fits the plan, and the opening odds show whether the swap changed how often you mulligan.

Planning a Deck Around Rotation

Rotation hits some decks much harder than others. A deck whose main attacker and draw engine all carry the oldest letter may need a full rebuild, while a deck that only loses a Tool or a Stadium needs a single swap. Start with the cards your deck cannot work without: the main attacker, the Pokémon it evolves from, and the cards that find them. If all of those are safe, the deck has a future after rotation. Rotation Questions

When is the next Pokémon TCG rotation?
It has not been announced yet. If it follows recent years, H will be announced in January and leave Standard at events in April 2027. RdyDeck updates this page when the official announcement comes.
Do Basic Energy cards rotate?
No. Basic Energy carries no regulation mark and stays legal in Standard.
Is a reprinted card still legal?
Yes, when a printing with the same name, attacks and Abilities carries a legal mark, every printing of that card is legal in Standard.
