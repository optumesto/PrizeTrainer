Why Prize Mapping Matters

Six cards start face down as prizes. If you know which ones, you know what you can still search for, which lines are open, and when a prize you take could give you a key card back. Working it out fast, while you set up, is a skill, and it gets faster with practice.

How a Training Game Works

  1. Import your deck, or pick a starter deck, and tap Prizes on it. You can also open the deck's Drills tab and tap Play under Prize game.
  2. RdyDeck sets cards aside as prizes: 6 by default, or 4 or 8.
  3. You flip through the rest of the deck.
  4. You name the cards that are missing, and RdyDeck shows how you did.

Add a time limit to make it feel like a real game. After a few games, the Progress screen shows which cards you miss most, so you know what to focus on.

What Prize Mapping Is

Prize mapping is working out which of your cards are in your six prizes. Players do it on their first turn, often while a search card has them looking through the deck anyway. Every card you do not see in your hand, on the board or in the deck must be a prize. Knowing that early changes your plan: if both copies of your main attacker's lower stage are prized, you set up a different attacker first and save the prize you take for when it can bring one back.

How to Map Your Prizes Faster

  • Check your single copies first: your ACE SPEC, one copy Tools, Stadiums and tech Pokémon. A single copy you cannot find is a prize, and these are the cards that change your plan the most.
  • Count by group: count your Pokémon, then your Supporters, then your Items and Energy, rather than the whole deck at once. Small groups are easier to hold in your head.
  • Learn your list cold: you can only spot what is missing if you know what should be there. Mapping is fastest with a list you have played many times.
  • Use the time you have: do it during a search you were going to make anyway, not as a separate step, so the game keeps moving.

Ways to Practice in RdyDeck

  • Prize count: play with 4, 6 or 8 prizes. Fewer is a good way to start, and more is a hard test.
  • Time limit: off, 1 minute, 90 seconds or 2 minutes, so practice feels like the clock at an event.
  • Table Check: holds out an opening hand of 7, so you map the prizes from the rest of the deck as you would at the table.
  • Reach Check: before the reveal, answer whether you can still get a card, the question that matters most in a real game.
  • Progress: after a few games, see your accuracy and the cards you miss most.

Prize Trainer Questions

How many prize cards are there in the Pokémon TCG?
Six in a standard game. Each player sets aside the top six cards of their deck after drawing their opening hand, and takes one when they Knock Out a Pokémon, or more for a Pokémon ex or a Mega Evolution Pokémon ex.
How often does a single copy of a card start in my prizes?
About one game in ten for a 60 card deck. The opening odds page has the numbers for 2, 3 and 4 copies.
Is the prize trainer free?
Yes. It is free, needs no account and works offline once RdyDeck has loaded.
