How to improve your Pokémon TCG deck

To improve a Pokémon TCG deck, check it in this order: every card is legal, every attacker can pay for its attack and every evolution has what it evolves from, the deck has a steady way to draw and search, its counts sit near what winning lists run, and it has a plan against the decks you will face. RdyDeck, a free deck checker, runs these checks for you: paste your decklist and it rates the deck out of 10 and names what to fix first.

1. Make sure every card is legal

A card that is not Standard legal makes the deck unplayable at an event, so fix this first. Check each card's regulation mark. An older printing is still legal when a reprint with the same name, attacks and Abilities is legal now. The rotation checker marks which cards are legal now and which may rotate next.

2. Give every card what it needs

Every attacker needs the Energy its attacks cost, and every evolution needs the Pokémon it evolves from. A Stage 2 line also wants Rare Candy or enough search to find both stages. These gaps are the most common reason a deck stalls, and they are easy to miss when you build from a list of favourite cards.

3. Build a steady way to draw and search

A deck needs to find the same few cards every game: draw Supporters, search Items, or a Pokémon with a draw or search Ability. Without one, even strong attackers sit in the deck. A deck with a draw engine but single copies of cards it needs often will still miss them, so run more copies of what you need every game and fewer one of a kind ideas.

5. Check your opening odds

How often you mulligan, start without your attacker or put a key card in your prizes is just maths on your list. Too few Basic Pokémon means more mulligans. One copy of a key card means it is often prized. Opening odds works these out for your exact list.

6. Plan for the decks you will face

A deck that wins the prize race against the most played decks will do well even if it loses to a rare one. Look at which decks are popular now, how your attacker trades against them, and whether a tech card would swing a common matchup. The deck guides list each top deck's record and matchups from real tournament games.

7. Practice with the deck

A better list only helps if you play it well. Knowing which cards went to your prizes changes what you search for. The prize trainer drills this with your own deck.

Common questions

Is there a free tool that rates my Pokémon deck?
Yes. RdyDeck rates a 60 card Pokémon TCG deck out of 10 for free, with no account. Paste a list from Pokémon TCG Live or Limitless TCG and it scores Synergy and Power, compares the deck with recent tournament results and lists what to fix first.
How many Pokémon, Trainers and Energy should a Pokémon deck have?
A deck has exactly 60 cards. In 132 recent top 16 lists the typical split was 20 Pokémon, 31 Trainers and 9 Energy, with the middle half of lists running 17 to 21 Pokémon, 28 to 33 Trainers and 8 to 13 Energy. The right split depends on the deck.
Why does my Pokémon deck keep losing?
The usual causes are an attacker that cannot pay for its attack, an evolution with nothing to evolve from, no steady way to draw cards, or too few Basic Pokémon to start well. A deck checker like RdyDeck names which of these your list has.
Does this work for Pokémon TCG Pocket?
No. RdyDeck rates 60 card decks for the Pokémon Trading Card Game, played with paper cards or in Pokémon TCG Live. Pokémon TCG Pocket decks have 20 cards and different rules.
