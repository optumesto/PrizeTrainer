Can Your Deck Win?

Pokémon TCG deck grader

Paste your list and RdyDeck grades it out of 10, then tells you what to fix first. It is free, there is no account, and it works offline.

Grade my deck

On the home screen, tap Import a deck, paste your list and save, then tap the deck to see its grade.

How the grade works

The grade has two parts.

  • Synergy checks that your cards support each other: every attacker can pay for its attacks, evolutions have what they evolve from, and the deck can draw and search.
  • Power checks that the plan can win: how hard it hits against the format's biggest Pokémon, how many prizes it gives up and how fast it sets up.

The grade is Synergy times Power, divided by 10, so the weaker part pulls the whole grade down. One part of Synergy, how tightly the list fits together, lowers the grade only when the fit is loose, so a focused list can grade a little above Synergy times Power. Each grade shows its own math and comes with a short list of what to fix first.

A deck with cards that are not Standard legal right now grades 4.9 at most, until those cards are swapped out. The rotation checker shows which ones.

What it reads, and what it does not

  • The grade reads your list alone, on your own device. How the deck does against the current meta is shown next to it, in Against the meta.
  • Card roles are read from card text. That is usually right and sometimes not, so read the findings and do not worry about the decimal place.
  • Lists from Pokémon TCG Live exports and Limitless TCG both work: one card per line with the count, name, set code and number.
  • Built-in starter decks let you try it before you paste your own list.

Against the meta

Under the grade, RdyDeck matches your deck to the closest deck in recent Limitless online tournaments, by the Pokémon it plays, and shows that deck’s win rate against each of the most played decks. If no deck is close, pick one yourself.

These are results for decks like yours, not your exact list, unless your list matches a list that placed card for card. Matchups with fewer than 20 games are grayed out, and the numbers update every week.

After the grade

The same deck can go straight into practice. Check its opening odds, then drill it with the prize trainer until you can name your prizes without thinking.
