How grading works

How RdyDeck Grades a Deck

RdyDeck grades a 60 card Pokémon TCG deck out of 10. This page explains every part of that number: what it reads, how much each part counts, and where it can be wrong.

The short version: Synergy asks whether your cards work together, Power asks whether the plan can win, and the grade is Synergy times Power, divided by 10. When your deck matches a deck from recent online and local in-person tournaments, part of the grade also comes from how that deck has actually done.

A deck with cards that are not Standard legal right now grades 4.9 at most, until those cards are swapped out. An older printing still counts as legal when a reprint with the same name, attacks and Abilities is legal now, so old art is fine.

Synergy: do the cards work together?

Synergy starts at 10 and loses points for each thing that stops the cards from supporting each other. RdyDeck reads every card's own text to work out what it needs and what it gives.

  • Card needs. Every attacker needs the Energy its attacks cost, and every evolution needs what it evolves from. Synergy drops in proportion to the needs your list does not meet.
  • A draw engine. A deck needs a steady way to find its cards: draw Supporters, or a Pokémon with a draw or search Ability. A list with no real draw engine has its Synergy cut to 40%, or only to 75% when it runs plenty of cards that search the deck, like Supporters that find Pokémon.
  • A clear attacker. If no Pokémon can do the attacking, Synergy is cut to 30%.
  • Problems that stop the deck. Some gaps stop the deck outright, such as a Stage 2 with nothing to evolve from. Each costs 1.2 more.
  • Trainers and Special Energy. RdyDeck checks Items, Supporters, Tools, Stadiums, Special Energy and your Ace Spec: is anything missing that the deck clearly wants, and is anything dead in this list? This can move Synergy by up to 35%.
  • How tightly the list fits. A list that spreads itself over too many ideas, or runs single copies of cards it needs often, plays less consistently. This lowers Synergy in full, but it only lowers the grade when the fit is loose. So a focused list can grade a little above Synergy times Power.

Not every deck can reach a perfect 10 in Synergy. Strong decks often accept a gap on purpose, for example a tech card that is dead in some matchups.

Power: can the plan win?

Power is made of three parts, each scored out of 10 and then weighted.

PartWeightWhat it measures
Damage50%How hard your best attacker hits the format's biggest threats, and how many hits it needs to take a Knock Out against 280 and 340 HP Pokémon. A hit that needs time to grow, like one that scales with Energy in play, counts at the size it reaches in a normal game.
Prize trade30%How many prizes your Pokémon give up when they are Knocked Out. Big two and three prize attackers are fine when they hit hard enough to win the trade.
Setup speed20%How fast the deck gets its attackers going. A Stage 2 line without Rare Candy, or with too few search cards, sets up slowly. A deck of Basics and Stage 1s has no Stage 2 line to wait on.

Why the Grade Multiplies

The grade is Synergy times Power, divided by 10. A deck with Synergy 9 and Power 9 grades 8.1. A deck with Synergy 9 and Power 5 grades 4.5, not 7. Multiplying means the weaker part pulls the whole grade down, which matches how decks play: a consistent deck that cannot win the prize race loses, and so does a powerful deck that cannot find its cards.

Both parts have a floor, 0.6 for Synergy and 1.2 for Power, so a broken list still gets a grade that tells you how far it has to go.

Tournament Results

Synergy and Power read your list alone. Real results add something card text cannot: how a deck actually does against the decks people play. So when your deck matches a deck from recent Limitless online and local in-person tournaments, by the Pokémon it plays, part of the grade comes from that deck's win rate and finishes.

  • How much it counts. More games mean more weight. Once a deck has 250 games, results and cards count evenly, half the grade each. Before that the share grows with the games: 20% at 100 games, 40% at 200. Games since the newest set count three times here too.
  • Newer games count more. Games since the newest set came out count three times, so the grade follows the format as it changes.
  • What an average record scores. A deck that wins as often as it loses scores 8.0 from results alone, since any deck that is played enough to have a record is already a real deck. A record below average counts twice as hard as one above it, so a deck that keeps losing grades low even when its cards read well.
  • When results are left out. A list that grades under 6 from its cards gets less weight from results, since it may share Pokémon with a good deck without playing like one. A list held at 4.9 for cards that are not legal gets none.

The results show how decks like yours have done, not your exact list. They update every week.

A Worked Example

Here is a Mega Rayquaza list that finished 5th in a Limitless online event on 9 Nov 2026. RdyDeck reads its cards at Synergy 8.0 and Power 8.1. Its Synergy loss comes from how tightly the list fits, which does not lower the grade when the fit is close, so the card grade is 10 times 8.1, divided by 10, which is 8.1. Mega Rayquaza had won 49.0% of 726 online games at the time, and that record alone scores 7.0. With games since the newest set counted three times, results make up about half of this grade, so the list's 8.1 becomes 7.6: Strong.

The app shows the same breakdown for your deck, in plain sentences, under every grade. See the Mega Rayquaza deck guide for more on how the deck plays.

What to Fix First

Each grade comes with a short list of findings. Each one names the cards involved and why it matters, for example an attacker that cannot pay for its attack, or a Stage 2 line with no Rare Candy. Fix the top finding, grade the deck again and watch the number move. RdyDeck can also suggest upgrades that keep your main attacker and the plan you built. To run the same checks by hand, see how to improve your deck.

Where the Grade Can Be Wrong

RdyDeck reads card roles from card text. That is usually right and sometimes not, especially for a card with an unusual effect or one that has just come out. So read the findings, not only the number, and do not worry about the decimal place. If a grade looks wrong for a list you know well, the Send feedback button in the app tells us, and those reports are how the grader gets better.

We check the grader against real results: it grades the same fixed set of top 16 lists from recent events after each change (counted in the next section), so a fix for one deck does not quietly break another.

How Accurate the Grades Are

Every week RdyDeck grades the same 132 top 16 lists from recent events. 130 of them are used here; the other 2 use cards that have rotated or older printings. RdyDeck checks the order its grades put those decks in against how the decks actually win in Limitless online tournaments.
